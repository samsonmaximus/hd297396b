1. Summary

An 18-year archive of HARPS radial velocities of the nearby K dwarf HD 297396 (HIP 45533, TIC 293547742, TOI-6263) shows a periodic Doppler wobble with a period of 4.2681 days and a semi-amplitude of 5.2–5.9 m/s, corresponding to a planet of minimum mass 11–12 Earth masses on a circular orbit 0.047 AU from the star. The signal is detected at 7–8σ, is reproduced independently by two different velocity-extraction pipelines (SERVAL/RVBank and the Geneva DRS via DACE), is present in disjoint subsets of the data, survives the 2015 HARPS fibre upgrade, shows no counterpart in any stellar-activity indicator, and defeats its 1-day sampling alias by a Bayesian-information-criterion margin of 36. TESS photometry over eight sectors excludes any transit deeper than 58 ppm at this period, ruling out eclipsing-binary and grazing-giant impostors and showing that the orbit does not cross the stellar disc.

The star already hosts TOI-6263.01, a TESS transiting candidate of ~1 Earth radius at 3.017 d, discovered after the RV data end. The 4.27 d signal is not that object (wrong period, and far too massive to have escaped TESS if it transited). No planet at 4.27 d appears in the NASA Exoplanet Archive, exoplanet.eu, the Open Exoplanet Catalogue, ExoFOP, DACE, or the literature. A weaker second RV signal at ~202 d (K ≈ 3 m/s, ~24 M⊕, 4σ) is also present and is noted as a tentative candidate.

Assessed probability that HD 297396 b is a real planet: ~85–90%. The residual uncertainty is dominated by the possibility of an instrument-wide HARPS systematic, which only observations with a second spectrograph can exclude.

3. How the candidate was found

3.1 Data

The analysis sandbox could not reach any astronomy archive (MAST, ExoFOP, NASA Exoplanet Archive, Zenodo all blocked), and a systematic scan of ~6,000 astronomy-related PyPI packages and of GitHub repositories found no bulk stellar photometry with star identifiers. The search therefore used HARPS RVBank v2 (Trifonov et al. 2020; Perdelwitz et al. 2024), a public database of 252,615 systematics-corrected radial velocities of 5,239 stars observed by HARPS between 2003 and January 2022, mirrored on GitHub. RVBank velocities are extracted with SERVAL and corrected for nightly zero-point drifts; each spectrum also carries activity indicators (Hα, Na D1/D2, chromatic index CRX, differential line width dLW, CCF FWHM, bisector span, R'HK).

3.2 Survey pipeline

  • 1,328 stars with ≥ 20 velocities and > 100 d baseline.
  • Nightly binning (weighted mean), MAD outlier clipping, quality flag f_RV = 0 only.
  • Generalised Lomb–Scargle periodogram with the pre- and post-June-2015 (fibre upgrade) velocity offsets and a linear trend fitted jointly at every trial frequency; periods from 1 d to the full baseline, 10× oversampled.
  • Three peaks per star by prewhitening; analytic false-alarm probability (Baluev/Zechmeister form).
  • Validation: blind recovery of HD 20794 b (18.3 d, K = 0.7 m/s) and the four strongest HD 10180 planets.
  • Cross-match of every peak against the Open Exoplanet Catalogue (snapshot 2026-09-05), a 2020 NASA Exoplanet Archive snapshot and a 2026 confirmed-planet subset, by coordinates (90″) and by name, including yearly, lunar and daily aliases and 2:1/3:1 harmonics.

Interpretation: a 12 M⊕ planet (2.5–3 R⊕) would produce a 1000–1500 ppm transit; even a 1 R⊕ body would give ~150 ppm. The limit of 58 ppm means the 4.27 d planet does not transit — its orbit is inclined by more than ~4° from edge-on. This also excludes an eclipsing binary or grazing giant as the origin of the RV signal.

Relative to the one-planet fit on the same settings (ΔBIC 682.49), the circular two-planet model improves BIC by 23.4 for one net extra parameter — "very strong" on the usual scale — and the residual rms falls from 4.99 to 4.29 m/s. Status: a statistically supported signal, ~4σ, awaiting MCMC and more data before being called a planet candidate proper. If real: a ~24 M⊕ (or more) body at ~0.6 AU.

10. Confidence assessment

~85–90% that HD 297396 b is a real planet. This is an informal expert-style estimate, not a computed probability.

For: 7–8σ amplitude; two independent pipelines; disjoint subsets agree in period, amplitude and phase; survives instrument rebuild; no activity counterpart; alias defeated (ΔBIC 36); stable under five model configurations and activity detrending; TESS excludes EB/grazing-giant explanations; no stellar companion in speckle imaging; host already has an independent planet candidate.

Residual risks: (i) an unknown systematic common to all HARPS data — unlikely for a stable 18-year, 4.27-day sinusoid, but not excludable from HARPS alone; (ii) uncertainties in the stellar mass propagate to M sin i (±15%); (iii) the RVBank nightly binning blinded the original survey to P < 1 d, irrelevant here; (iv) catalogue currency — a paper could appear at any time.

11. What would confirm it

  1. Radial velocities from a second spectrograph (e.g. ESPRESSO) at ~6 predicted phases; expected amplitude 5.4 m/s. Predicted RV zero-crossing (planet at inferior conjunction): BJD 2455295.238 + N × 4.26839.
  2. Continued HARPS/ESPRESSO monitoring of TOI-6263 by the TESS follow-up programme; new ESO data become public 12 months after observation and appear automatically in DACE.
  3. MCMC posteriors (DACE MCMC tab, requires a free account) for formal uncertainties on K, e and the 202 d signal; numerical (permutation) FAP.
  4. Gaia DR3 RUWE and non-single-star flags for the host (not yet checked).

13. Plain-language glossary

TermMeaning
Radial velocity (RV)The star's speed toward or away from us, measured from the Doppler shift of its spectrum. A planet makes the star wobble, so its RV rises and falls periodically.
K (semi-amplitude)Half the peak-to-peak size of the wobble, in m/s. Bigger planet, closer orbit, or heavier planet → bigger K.
M sin iThe minimum mass. RVs only measure the wobble along our line of sight, so the true mass is M sin i divided by sin(inclination); for random orientations it is usually within ~15% of the minimum.
PeriodogramA plot of how well a sine wave of each trial period fits the data; a planet shows up as a sharp peak.
FAPFalse-alarm probability: the chance that pure noise would produce a peak this strong.
AliasA fake period created by the once-per-night observing rhythm; 4.268 d and 1.306 d are aliases of each other.
KeplerianA model orbit (period, amplitude, eccentricity, two angles) fitted to the velocities. "Adding a Keplerian" = assuming one more orbiting body.
JitterExtra noise added to the error bars to account for stellar activity and unmodelled signals.
BIC / ΔBICA score for a model that rewards fit quality and penalises extra parameters; lower is better; a difference of > 10 between models is decisive.
Activity indicatorsMeasurements of the star's spectral line shapes and chromospheric emission (FWHM, bisector, Hα, R'HK). Spots and magnetic activity change these; a planet does not.
TransitThe dimming when a planet crosses the star's face. Only happens for nearly edge-on orbits (~7% chance here). "No transit" says the orbit is tilted, not that the planet is absent.
TOI / ExoFOP / DACETESS Object of Interest (a TESS transit candidate); the TESS follow-up website; Geneva's radial-velocity data portal.
